# The specialist summary: php2shell, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619

On 21 October 2026 at 14:00 UTC the PHP project released fixes for two critical flaws, **CVE-2026-109618** and **CVE-2026-109619**, nicknamed *php2shell*. Either can give an unauthenticated attacker remote code execution on a web server running PHP, the language behind WordPress, Drupal, Wikipedia and a large share of the web. The flaws were found with AI help, the coordinated disclosure broke down two days early, and by the morning of 22 October the NSA, CISA and the UK's NCSC were telling organisations that could not patch to take systems offline. This site follows the story as evidence: every claim points to a captured, dated and hashed source, every number says what it measures, and every contradiction between sources is left visible.

## What is established
- There are two flaws, both in the PHP engine, both rated CVSS v4.0 9.2, both fixed in PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35. The CVE records, the GitHub advisories and the php.net advisory agree on this. [CVE record](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/cve-cve-2026-109618/) [CVE record](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/cve-cve-2026-109619/) [PHP](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/github-ghsa-w8hw-w9fx-j79w/) [PHP](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/github-ghsa-6wmf-4rv5-p3rp/) [php.net](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/php-net-advisory/)
- Unauthenticated remote code execution was demonstrated against Apache with mod_php before any application code runs. PHP-FPM is susceptible; the two primary accounts differ on whether a complete FPM exploit exists. [PHP](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/github-ghsa-w8hw-w9fx-j79w/) [oss-security](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/openwall-oss-security-2026-10-21-6/) [PHP Foundation](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/php-foundation-blog-security-release/)
- The flaws are old. The out-of-memory problem behind CVE-2026-109618 was first seen during a 2024 audit (Quarkslab, funded through OSTIF) and left unfixed because nobody could prove exploitation. PHP 8.1 and older are out of support; the project published minimal source backports for 7.4, 8.0 and 8.1, but the CVE records list only 8.2 to 8.5. [PHP Foundation](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/php-foundation-blog-security-release/) [Calif](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/calif-php2shell/) [php-src](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/github-php-src-branch-7.4-backport/) [CVE record](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/cve-cve-2026-109618/)
- Discovery was AI-assisted and three parties claim it. Calif reported first (hash on 5 August, report on 11 August); OpenAI reported the same root cause the same day; Wiz found it independently on 19 August and reported on 24 August. The GitHub advisories credit four reporters. - Disclosure broke down on 20 October around 15:00 UTC, when the CVE IDs circulated with a wrong CVSS of 10.0. ## What is not

Open questions, stated rather than papered over. Each one names what would close it.
- Who first leaked the CVE IDs on 20 October, and where. The PHP Foundation does not say; a PHP security team member points to an anonymous Hacker News post, and Notebookcheck and Nguoi Quan Sat repeat that account. No copy of the post is in the vault.
- Whether the deleted Calif video is the one now embedded on Calif's own write-up. Calif's page does not mention the leak.
- Whether any site was actually compromised before it was patched or taken offline. Every claim of exploitation so far is of attempts, probes or honeypot hits, or, in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities listing of 26 October, of exploitation with no victim named; or, in Dream Research's report of 26 October, of 25 rooted servers with no victim or vendor named and no confirmation (one source, repeated by three outlets); the named cases do not state php2shell as the cause. Authorities in Singapore and Luxembourg now ask operators to look for traces; the answer will come from those searches, if it comes.
- Which sites were really taken offline, and why. The list now draws on more captured reports (Germany's hospitals and hosts, Morocco, Romania, Japan), but most are press accounts; few come from the organisations themselves (the Dutch government, Latvia's agency, Weird Gloop, the Charité's spokesman as quoted). A community tracker counts 51 listed and 50 back.
- Whether the 8.5.12 fix is complete. A Fediverse report of a third bug or incomplete fix reached oss-security on 21 October; PHP reportedly told Canonical it is unrelated. On 23 October PHP published an informational advisory for a separate flaw, CVE-2026-115560, in the session upload progress feature, reported by Cloudflare, SOCRadar and The Hacker News: it affects fully patched releases including 8.5.12, needs a rare session setting, will be fixed in the next regular release, and has session.upload_progress.enabled=0 as the mitigation; SOCRadar reports no exploitation and no severity score. PHP's advisory is not in the evidence vault, and whether this is the bug the Fediverse report described is not stated.
- What the 70% figure is measured from. None of the three pages that carry it names the measurement.
- The model and the method. Calif says no special harness and names no model; OpenAI's blog, which we could not capture, reportedly names GPT-5.6 Sol; Wiz names Gemini 4 Argon, GPT-5.6 Sol and Wiz Atlas. What did the models do and what did the people do?
- OpenAI's own blog post, the Patch the Planet launch post, the Spanish and Portuguese advisories and the Techzine piece on Chainguard are all blocked by bot challenges from this capture environment. The block is recorded; the content is not read.
