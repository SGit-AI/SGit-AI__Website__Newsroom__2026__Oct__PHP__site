Question · kept by the librarian · librarian@php.newsroom

Has anyone actually been hacked?

How it was asked: The Times' standfirst: 'Hackers could seize control of online stores and steal passwords'.

The short answer

Not that any named organisation has said, as of 17:13 UTC on 29 October, the latest capture in the evidence vault. Attack attempts are real and in the millions at Cloudflare alone, Amazon saw one on a honeypot four days before disclosure, and two security vendors are reported as saying it is being exploited, without naming any victim (other reports of the same vendors describe attempts). CISA added both flaws to its exploited-vulnerabilities list on 26 October, which means it has evidence of exploitation, but it names no victim. One security company, Dream Research, reports that a campaign using the flaws rooted 25 servers, three of them in a US government-affiliated environment, but it names no victim and no vendor, nobody has confirmed it and The Next Web says it has not verified it: reported, not confirmed. A researcher's deliberately vulnerable decoys were broken into, which proves the attack works but names no victim. The sites that went offline say they did it as a precaution.

To paste back where it was asked: the paragraph above and https://php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/questions/been-hacked/index.html

The answer, with its sources

Attempts: yes. Cloudflare's chief executive reported millions of exploit attempts across its network by the early hours of 22 October; Amazon saw an attempt on a honeypot on 17 October; Latvia saw automated attempts, none successful. Matthew Prince, Cloudflare CEO on… Amazon CERT.LV

Successful break-ins: none named. As of 17:13 UTC on 29 October no named organisation has said it was compromised through php2shell, and the five named cases in the press (Valtec, ČVUT, GMO, DMM, and Square Enix, which an Italian site reports confirmed unauthorised access to its systems) either were retracted or state no php2shell cause; two security vendors, Hadrian and Patchstack, are reported as saying it is being exploited, without naming a victim, while other reports of the same vendors describe attack attempts, not compromises. CISA's alert of 21 October said it had not observed exploitation; on 26 October CISA added both CVEs to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, which takes evidence of exploitation, naming no victim: exploited, but no organisation named as compromised. A researcher's decoy servers, left on the vulnerable PHP 8.4.26 on purpose, were broken into: 35 working shells under Apache mod_php (Adel Karimi of Perplexity, reported on 26 October): proof the attack works, on a decoy, not a victim. Palo Alto's Unit 42 withdrew, on 25 October, a statement tying an attack on a European government agency to php2shell, and on 26 October watchTowr's answer to whether reliable exploitation was happening in the wild was 'a firm No'. Huntress saw none on its partners' systems. Craft CMS says there is evidence the flaws were exploited before the fixes were released, without saying whose or naming a victim; Craft (for the sites it hosts), NETGEAR, Broadcom, Arista, Splunk and Salesforce say they know of no successful exploitation or compromise of their own systems, devices or customers. One security company, Dream Research, reported on 26 October that a campaign using the flaws, with a Linux kernel flaw, rooted 25 servers, three of them in a US government-affiliated environment; it names no victim and withholds the firewall vendor and its evidence, and cannot tell whether its later steps worked. The Hacker News, The Next Web and CyPro repeat it as one source, The Next Web says it has not independently verified it, and no victim, vendor, CISA or PHP-team statement confirms it: reported, not confirmed, and not a named organisation saying it was compromised. This is a statement about the evidence so far, and it will change. CISA CISA CISA Huntress ENTER.CO SC Media The Register Insurance Business Security.NL BleepingComputer INTERNET Watch Lupa.cz Yahoo! The offline sites were precautions. Weird Gloop, the Dutch government's DigiD and the NCSC-NL itself, Finland's Valtori and Oxford City Council all describe taking sites down to be safe, not after a breach. A UK minister said there was no breach of Budget security after the OBR site was affected. Weird Gloop Rijksoverheid / NCSC-NL Yle Oxford Chronicle East Anglian Daily Times

The caveat. A clean log does not prove a clean system (JPCERT), and the last PHP core flaw, in 2024, was exploited one day after disclosure. Absence of a confirmed victim after two days is a fact about two days; authorities in Singapore and Luxembourg now ask operators to look for traces of a break-in.
