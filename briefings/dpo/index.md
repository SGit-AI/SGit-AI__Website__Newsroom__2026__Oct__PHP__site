# Was personal data exposed, does this start a GDPR breach assessment or a 72-hour notification, and what do I document?

M
The answer in thirty seconds

A flaw is not a breach. An unpatched, internet-facing PHP application holding personal data is a risk to record and treat; it becomes a breach to assess when there is a sign of compromise on it. No named organisation has said it was compromised through php2shell as of the last check. Ask your hosts in writing, review from 17 October, and document the assessment either way.

[Read the site as Morgan →](../../compromise/index.html)

Four questions, in order: is personal data on a system that was exposed, has anything happened to it, does that make it a personal data breach, and what goes on the record. A vulnerability is not a breach, and an outage you chose is not one either; a sign that someone ran code on a system holding personal data is where the assessment starts. Written by the GRC desk; the full register is at [GRC](../../grc/index.html).

## What to decide or do
- **Find the personal data first.** Ask the CTO or security lead for the list of internet-facing PHP systems (vendor products and hosting panels count), and mark which hold or reach personal data: accounts, orders, case files, the database the application logs into. Code execution on the server gives an attacker what the PHP process can read, usually including the database credentials in its configuration.
- **Exposed is not breached.** An unpatched system is a risk under Article 32 to treat and record, not a breach under Article 33. As of the last check, no named organisation has said it was compromised through php2shell; attempts are widely reported and two vendors report successful exploitation without naming a victim. [What that statement rests on](../../compromise/index.html).
- **When the 72-hour clock starts.** Under Article 33 it runs from when you become aware, with reasonable certainty, that personal data was affected: a web shell, a PHP worker spawning a shell, an unexplained database export on a system holding personal data. Ask the security lead to tell you the hour that happens, not the day after, and start the assessment then.
- **A precautionary shutdown is a decision, not a breach.** Taking a service offline on purpose to protect it is not an accidental or unlawful loss of availability. Record who decided, when, for how long, and that no personal data was lost.
- **Ask your processors in writing.** A host or managed platform is a processor under Article 28 and must tell you of a breach without undue delay (Article 33(2)). Ask each one: have you updated PHP on the systems that process our data, since when, have you seen any sign of compromise, and will you tell us within hours if you do? Keep the replies.
- **Document either way.** Article 33(5) asks you to record every breach; accountability under Article 5(2) asks you to show your reasoning when you decide something is not one. Record: the systems and the personal data on each, the date each was patched or isolated, the log review window (from 17 October, the earliest reported attempt, not from the 21st), what was found, and the decision with its reasons and who made it.
- **If it becomes a breach.** Notify the supervisory authority within 72 hours unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to people (Article 33), and tell the people affected without undue delay if the risk to them is high (Article 34). Coordinate with whoever owns the NIS2 or DORA report: the same incident can start a 24-hour clock there.

## What is still open
- Who first leaked the CVE IDs on 20 October, and where. The PHP Foundation does not say; a PHP security team member points to an anonymous Hacker News post, and Notebookcheck and Nguoi Quan Sat repeat that account. No copy of the post is in the vault.
- Whether the deleted Calif video is the one now embedded on Calif's own write-up. Calif's page does not mention the leak.
- Whether any site was actually compromised before it was patched or taken offline. Every claim of exploitation so far is of attempts, probes or honeypot hits, or, in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities listing of 26 October, of exploitation with no victim named; the named cases do not state php2shell as the cause. Authorities in Singapore and Luxembourg now ask operators to look for traces; the answer will come from those searches, if it comes.
- Which sites were really taken offline, and why. The list now draws on more captured reports (Germany's hospitals and hosts, Morocco, Romania, Japan), but most are press accounts; few come from the organisations themselves (the Dutch government, Latvia's agency, Weird Gloop, the Charité's spokesman as quoted). A community tracker counts 51 listed and 50 back.
- Whether the 8.5.12 fix is complete. A Fediverse report of a third bug or incomplete fix reached oss-security on 21 October; PHP reportedly told Canonical it is unrelated. On 23 October PHP published an informational advisory for a separate flaw, CVE-2026-115560, in the session upload progress feature, reported by Cloudflare, SOCRadar and The Hacker News: it affects fully patched releases including 8.5.12, needs a rare session setting, will be fixed in the next regular release, and has session.upload_progress.enabled=0 as the mitigation; SOCRadar reports no exploitation and no severity score. PHP's advisory is not in the evidence vault, and whether this is the bug the Fediverse report described is not stated.
